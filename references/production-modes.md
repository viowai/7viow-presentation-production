# Production modes

Choose according to the requested division of authorship and current confirmation stage. Reuse the accepted mode. Changing it preserves unaffected decisions but cannot bypass required structure/content or producer-handoff approval.

## Supplied-content production

Give the producer the actual manuscript or page units, their responsibilities, visible copy, speaker support, real assets, and applicable structure or design instructions. Keep a page's related content adjacent when the user needs a page-by-page handoff. Distinguish mandatory decisions from optional advice.

The producer may make allowed layout changes while preserving required meaning. A content-capacity problem returns to the content owner for a faithful split, merge or rewrite; it does not license deleting qualifiers or service scope. An authorized local wording edit need not reopen the whole manuscript.

Review against the manuscript and retained page decisions. Completion requires the requested pages in the actual artifact, with usable editable content and required supporting material—not a production brief alone.

## Autonomous creation from context

Give enough context to make creative decisions: the reader's task, supported project facts and their relationships, priorities and why they matter, available evidence and assets, decisions to retain, and what changed this round. Explain the role of old material. During planning the creator can propose narrative, page order and copy. After stage-3 approval, production preserves accepted structure and meaning while choosing visual expression and permitted wording.

An initial user-supplied page script is not a prerequisite for architecture planning; the full proposed structure/content still goes through stage-3 confirmation before native production. Creative freedom is not a reason to omit factual context. Avoid inserting internal execution instructions or audit reports into audience copy.

Review against source meaning, preserved instructions, audience purpose and explanatory completeness. A different page count is acceptable unless the request fixes it; an unexplained omission is not. An overview that names stages does not explain the mechanisms later pages rely on.

## Meaning and narrative obligations

Judge what the reader can understand or decide, not whether every topic label appeared. Supply complete context and explain why each priority matters; creative freedom does not replace needed facts.

Give each page a communication task. Put a claim with the evidence and qualifications that support it. Connect chapters so the next section builds on the understanding established by the previous one. Repeated material must add a comparison, recovery of an earlier point or a new implication, rather than merely changing its heading.

Keep the complete set of accepted values and necessary conditions. A local storyline does not authorize reducing the whole project's value to one slogan. Identify where unique source meaning goes after a split, merge or move. Let page count follow sufficient explanation; do not copy an earlier deck's page slots, chapter quota or proprietary business facts.

## Batches and multiple versions

Use coherent units based on dependencies and editing load. A representative page may settle a visual uncertainty, but is not a universal approval gate. Within explicitly approved serial production or named level skips, continue ordinary operations. Serial scheduling alone does not waive the confirmation levels in confirmation-workflow.md.

Different audiences or creative routes have separate purposes and originals. Convert content for each reader's questions, responsibilities and next action, with shared facts remaining consistent. Do not silently merge an unaccepted version into another. A common source does not mean identical slides or identical disclosure.

Prefer explanation-bearing diagrams, readable tables and concrete page content over repeated decorative cards. Use the chosen presentation method for execution; this skill does not prescribe a universal visual template, slide quota or text-reduction ratio.
