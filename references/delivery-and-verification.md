# Delivery and verification

Read before accepting returned work, integrating a complete deck or claiming delivery. Verify the actual requested artifact and relevant surfaces. Choose checks that can detect a concrete failure and change the next action.

## Verification layers

1. **Content:** compare actual visible text, page order, notes and necessary qualifications against the correct mode's baseline. Check missing meaning, stronger claims, internal instructions in audience copy and material moved to the wrong surface. Text extraction helps locate content; it does not prove visibility.
2. **Rendered pages:** inspect the complete requested deck, including ordinary and dense pages, not only the cover. Check hierarchy, readable type, real diagram relationships, tables, cropping, overflow and page joins. Use the stated audience task to judge whether the page explains something.
3. **Native objects:** verify required text, diagrams, tables and charts are editable. Retain actual source artwork when required. Whole-slide images do not satisfy editable delivery. Inspect embedded media, links, notes and hidden slides when relevant.
4. **Target application and exports:** when compatibility is promised, actually open the native deck in that application and inspect relevant rendering or playback. State which exporter produced the PDF. A PDF cannot demonstrate video playback; a library render cannot demonstrate PowerPoint or Keynote compatibility.
5. **Delivery and disclosure:** open the actual paths, match the intended version and identify what to edit next. For audience-specific outputs, inspect the content and attachments included in that output, including notes and embedded objects when the disclosure requirements apply.

Report unchecked layers as unchecked. A tool's success message, a previous run, or one passed layer is not a substitute for the others. Fix observed failures, then recheck the changed scope and affected dependencies. Do not repeat every check after an unrelated punctuation repair.

For a diagram, read its connectors and grouping as explicit claims: what precedes what, what belongs to a whole, and where a correction returns. Compare those claims with the source meaning. A set of deliverables must not accidentally become a process chain; an expanded sub-process must remain attached to its parent; a feedback path must visibly reach the record it changes. Fix the relation even when every label is correct and nothing overlaps. A later visual review is not a substitute for this check before accepting the return.

## Reader-facing and maintenance content

Audience copy must answer that reader's task, even in a complete version used by the author. Speaker notes are also a delivery surface: use them for speaking support and meaningful evidence explanations, not automatic build paths, review routing, release controls, source IDs or internal status labels. Keep that production provenance in a separate maintenance record, rather than repeating it in every slide's notes. Necessary claims, limitations and source attribution remain accessible where they help the reader or speaker understand the evidence.

If a repeated internal field appears through a common generator, fix the owning data/projection or shared component and inspect the resulting deck and derivatives. A visibility toggle or one manually corrected page does not repair a repeated source error. A specific forbidden field/path warrants a targeted regression fixture when a build chain exists.

## Source and export lineage

Prefer one current editable source and regenerate its representations. If an authorized delivery-copy repair is necessary for a target application, distinguish the platform source from that repaired editable delivery copy and the PDF generated from it. Record the localized repair and specify the source for the next edit.

Returning to the platform source requires checking whether the delivery repair must be reapplied. Continuing locally uses the repaired source. Do not silently overwrite it with an older platform export or label both files simply “latest”. A repair changing meaning returns to the content owner.

## Delivery note

Lead with the real files and their uses, important changes, actual verification and remaining gaps. Keep the full operational history elsewhere. Identify whether the request is complete, a specific output is partial, or a return remains unverified. Explain the concrete next action, not an internal status command.

Production completion does not authorize distribution or prove business results. If only a brief was requested, delivering the brief can complete that task; do not pretend it was sent or that a deck returned.
